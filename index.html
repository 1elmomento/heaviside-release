Friendship

The reason Heaviside exists.

Friendships rarely end in an argument. They end in silences nobody chose. Heaviside shows you where each one stands, so you notice before the silence becomes the story.

Garden

Every friendship, a tree grown from its own history.

The trunk is as broad as the years you've known each other, each branch a year, the crown as full as the last three months. The season says where it stands: blooming, needs water, fading, resting — or, for someone gone, a moss-grown stump kept as a memory.

Water a friendship from its card, and the tree shows whether they answered.

Circles

Your closest five, fifteen and fifty.

Everyone you talk with, in rings by how many days you've been in touch over the past year — the circles the anthropologist Robin Dunbar found in every human network. Drag the slider back a quarter at a time and watch people move between them.

Magnetism

Whose messages bring you straight back.

How much you talk with someone is one thing. How strongly a message from them draws you back to the chat is another. Magnetism maps both — the quiet friend you always answer at once, and the busy chat you mostly answer from habit.

Wrapped

Your year in chats, told as a story.

Eleven slides: the people you talked with most, the friend you found, the one you drifted from, your longest streak, the hour you're most alive. Save any slide as a picture — it's yours to share, or to keep.

One page per person

A friendship, on one page.

Their tree, where you stand, your circle quarter by quarter, how you talk and when, and the milestones along the way. For someone no longer in your life, it reads as a memory.

Habits

Awards for how everyone chats — the Flash, the Double-Texter, the Night Owl. You're in the running too.

Chemistry

Who you're most in step with: the same hours, answers as quick, messages as long.

River

Your closest friendships as streams through the months, swelling and thinning.

Trends

Messages sent and received over time, who's rising, who's fading, who's gone quiet.

Built on research

Measured, not guessed.

Every verdict in the garden comes from published work on how relationships grow and fade — and each rule was checked against what real chats actually did next, before it was allowed into the app.

  • Closeness — rings of about 5, 15, 50 and 150, measured in days in contact, as Dunbar's layers are.
  • Silences — how often a silence this long ever ended, across all your chats: a survival estimate, and a verdict like gone only on enough evidence.
  • Fading — change points in how much you talk, so the garden can say not just that a friendship is fading, but since when.
  • Family is different: kin don't drift apart through silence the way friends do, so family never wilts.
  • Magnetism — a relational event model: each time you write, why that chat, out of all you could have written to.

Reader

A channel, read as the blog it was written as.

The author's hashtags become categories. Long pieces published in parts — Telegram caps a post at 4,096 characters — are put back together and read as one article, in a measured column with a real reading face, night, sepia or day.

Highlight any words, write notes on them, keep pieces for later, export to HTML, Markdown or PDF. Four Persian reading faces are built in, and right-to-left text is set paragraph by paragraph.

Your account, tidied

The noise, cleared in a click.

A manager for your account, not another chat app: pick chats, and act on all of them at once.

Bulk actions

Leave forty dead groups, mute every channel, mark everything read. You see what will happen first, it goes at a safe pace, and most of it can be undone.

Search everything

Every saved message, instantly and offline — phrases, exclusions, by chat, sender and date, with Persian and Arabic spellings folded together.

Feed and collections

Read many channels as one stream, group chats your own way, and let Worth a look find the quiet channels and inactive groups for you.

Questions

Before you download

Is it safe for my Telegram account?

Heaviside signs in once, like Telegram's own apps, and reuses that session rather than signing in again. It paces every request it makes, asks Telegram only for what the saved copy can't answer, and anything that changes your account — leaving, muting, marking as read — shows you what will happen and asks first.

Does anything get sent anywhere?

No. Your history is saved in a database on your computer, and the friendship models are worked out there. The app talks to Telegram and nothing else: no servers of ours, no analytics, no tracking.

Does it read my messages?

The friendship models use only who wrote and when. Some counts — words, emoji, laughs — are taken from the text on your computer for the Habits awards, and only the counts are kept. No AI reads your messages, here or anywhere.

Will it work where Telegram is blocked?

Turn on your VPN. Heaviside tries every way to Telegram at once — your system's proxy settings, a VPN app's local proxy (v2rayN, Nekoray, Hiddify, Clash), or a direct connection through a VPN that carries all traffic — and uses the first that answers. You can also set a proxy of your own.

Do I need API keys?

No. Heaviside has its own, so you sign in with your phone number only. If you'd rather use keys of your own from my.telegram.org, choose Use your own API keys on the sign-in screen.

Windows or macOS warns me when I open it

Heaviside isn't signed with a paid certificate yet, so the system can't vouch for it. On Windows, choose More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, open it once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.

What will it cost?

Nothing. Heaviside is free: no paid version, no account with us, no ads.

Is Heaviside made by Telegram?

No. It's an independent app that uses Telegram's public API to work with your own account. It isn't made or endorsed by Telegram.

Why "Heaviside"?

After Oliver Heaviside, who worked out the mathematics of the telegraph line — how a signal keeps its shape over a long wire. The logo's crossbar is his step function.
